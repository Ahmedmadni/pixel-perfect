# Architecture rules
- Features live in `src/features/<name>/{components,hooks,services,schemas,lib}`; routes stay thin. Why: keeps pages free of logic.
- All data access goes through feature services that throw `DataProviderError` (`DATA_PROVIDER_NOT_CONNECTED` when no DB). Why: DB hookup is integration-only, no mock data.
- Odometer rules live in `src/features/odometer/lib/odometer-rules.ts`; DB trigger mirrors them. Why: single source of business logic.
- Prepared SQL lives in `database/migrations/` until Cloud is enabled. Why: supabase/migrations is tool-managed.
- The standard OBD-II library is a shared read-only table (`obd_fault_codes`) seeded from the bundled `data/obd-codes.json` by `scripts/import-obd-codes.mjs` (upsert on `code`; `--refresh` re-pulls GitHub); users add or override codes in per-user `user_obd_codes`, merged via `mergeObdCodes` (user wins) everywhere codes are looked up. Why: no repeated downloads, idempotent import, and custom codes reach reports and AI analysis.
- Symptom history links and due-repair alerts share the pure vehicle-scoped history-links rule; text matches are suggestions, and resolved issues never drive due-repair alerts. Why: the symptoms page and dashboard must agree without treating catalog symptoms as actual faults.
- Comprehensive maintenance reports load through a report feature service and share vehicle-scoped pure calculations; linked installations are displayed separately and excluded from the service-plus-independent-parts total with an explicit inclusion assumption. Why: synchronized expenses and associated parts must not silently duplicate service costs.
- OBD code-to-part links are per-user rows (obd_code_parts) limited to the user's own parts; AI recommendations get parts attached deterministically via the AI's related_codes plus vehicle fitment, never AI-invented parts. Why: suggestions must come only from the user's real parts catalog.
